HEVC, also called H.265, compresses video roughly twice as efficiently as H.264: a similar quality at around half the file size. It is the codec iPhones record in and plays natively on Apple devices and most modern hardware.

## Why convert videos to HEVC (H.265)?

Use HEVC to save storage or bandwidth, especially for Apple-centric audiences. HEVC encoding depends on your browser and hardware: it works in Safari and in Chrome or Edge on devices with HEVC support. If it is not available, the converter shows an error and you can choose MP4 (H.264) instead.

## Frequently asked questions

<FAQ question="Why does HEVC conversion fail on my computer?">
  Browsers can only encode HEVC when the operating system or graphics hardware
  provides an encoder. Try Safari on a Mac, or Chrome or Edge on a recent
  device, or pick H.264 MP4.
</FAQ>

<FAQ question="Will HEVC videos play on websites?">
  Safari plays HEVC everywhere; Chrome and Edge play it on devices with hardware
  support; Firefox support is limited. For websites, offer H.264 MP4 or WebM as
  a fallback.
</FAQ>

<FAQ question="Are my videos uploaded?">
  No. The video is decoded and encoded in your browser, on your device. Nothing
  is sent to a server, which also means there is no file size limit other than
  your device memory.
</FAQ>
