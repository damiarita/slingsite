# TinyPNG vs. Slingsite: Breaking Free from Compression Limits

TinyPNG is a household name in image optimization. It’s simple, effective, and pioneered the drag-and-drop compression experience. But in 2026, many webmasters and developers are finding that TinyPNG’s "freemium" model is getting in the way of their workflow.

If you’re tired of hitting the 20-image-per-session limit or dealing with 5MB file size caps, it might be time to switch. Here is how **Slingsite** compares to TinyPNG.

## TinyPNG: The Traditional Approach

TinyPNG relies on a server-side model. You upload your files to their cloud, their servers process them, and you download the optimized version.

### The Good

- **Simplicity:** It’s famous for being extremely easy to use.
- **Integrations:** Offers plugins for Photoshop and various CMS platforms.

### The "Pain Points"

- **The "Quota Wall":** The most frustrating part of TinyPNG is the limit. Once you hit 20 images, you have to refresh or stop. For a project with 50+ images, this becomes a major bottleneck.
- **File Size Caps:** The 5MB limit on the free tier prevents you from optimizing high-resolution photography or raw assets.
- **Privacy Concerns:** Since your images are sent to a third-party server, it’s not the best choice for proprietary or sensitive assets that shouldn't leave your machine.

---

## Slingsite: The Modern, Client-Side Alternative

[Slingsite](https://slingsite.github.io) takes a different approach. It performs all compression locally in your browser. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is stored, and nothing is tracked.

### The Advantage

- **No "Pro" Version:** You get the full power of the tool without ever seeing a subscription pop-up.
- **Truly Unlimited:** No session caps, no image count limits, and no file size hurdles.
- **Video Included:** Unlike TinyPNG, which focuses purely on images, Slingsite handles video optimization. This is a massive time-saver for modern sites heavy on video backgrounds or media.
- **Privacy-First:** Because the processing is done on _your_ CPU, your data never leaves your device. It is the safest way to handle work-in-progress content.
